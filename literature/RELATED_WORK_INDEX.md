## Why each one matters

**Fuoli et al. 2025 (9.5, Essential).** The closest methodological precedent for this thesis: full-text metaphor identification (not pre-selected single words) with three LLM deployment strategies (RAG, prompt engineering, fine-tuning) across 10 models, reporting precision/recall/F1 and a detailed human-LLM discrepancy taxonomy. Best F1 = 0.79 (fine-tuned GPT-4.1 mini). Directly useful as a template for evaluation design, error-taxonomy construction, and the argument that LLM-human discrepancies map onto known theoretical grey areas rather than random noise.

**Reimann & Scheffler 2025 (9.0, Essential).** A negative result: zero/few-shot LLaMA and GPT models fail at (extended) metaphor detection, with LLaMA showing severe overgeneralization. Important counterweight to Fuoli et al.'s optimism — shows the picture is method- and setup-dependent (full-text + fine-tuning/RAG works much better than token-level zero-shot). Useful for calibrating expectations and for an error-type taxonomy (overinterpretation, domain confusion, wrong boundaries).

**Al Kaabi et al. 2024 (8.5, Essential).** The only found work combining ChatGPT + classical Arabic rhetoric + comparison against a named classical scholarly tradition (al-Jurjani's *Dala'il al-I'jaz*). Qualitative (8 examples), not a benchmark, but it is the most topically adjacent non-DHQ precedent: it finds ChatGPT gives "basic," surface-level interpretations of metaphor/simile/metonymy but misses cultural/psychological depth — directly supports the "LLMs on Arabic rhetoric is under-studied and what exists is small-scale and qualitative" framing. Caveat: several of its own cited sources (e.g. "Smith 2022," "Jones 2020," "Margolis 2019" in vaguely named journals) look unverifiable/possibly fabricated — treat the paper's own literature review with caution, though its own methodology and findings are independently assessable.

**Hicke & Kristensen-McLachlan 2024 (8.0, Strong).** Already cited inside the Abd Alhadi/Gutman notes chain as a DHQ-adjacent precedent (Fuoli et al. also cite it). Tests GPT-3.5/4/4o on MIP-based metaphor identification via a single operationalized prompt; found LLMs can replicate the MIP procedure's reasoning steps with "surprising depth." Useful for prompt design (direct MIP operationalization) and as a second confirmed instance of multi-model GPT comparison.

**Balestrucci et al. 2025 (7.5, Strong).** Fine-tunes open-weight multilingual LLMs (Qwen, Llama, Ministral, LLaMAntino, Minerva) for rhetorical-figure classification (analogy, hyperbole, euphemism, rhetorical question, context shift, false assertion, oxymoron/paradox) in ironic Italian text, with human-vs-model Krippendorff's α reported (0.21–0.26, "fair" agreement — notably lower than human-human agreement of ~0.60–0.65). This is the closest non-Arabic precedent for "LLM classifies named rhetorical figures against a human-annotated scheme" — directly useful as a comparison point for expected agreement levels, though it covers irony-specific figures rather than classical rhetoric broadly, and generation rather than pure identification is a secondary focus.

**Ye et al. 2026 (7.0, Strong).** Not a benchmark paper but a diagnostic/skeptical one: argues strong LLM metaphor-task accuracy may reflect "heterogeneous underlying signals" (lexical anchoring, syntactic heuristics) rather than robust semantic understanding, via geometric probing and perturbation tests across 7 LLMs. Useful as a methodological caution for how to interpret high F1 scores in this thesis, and for the general point that behavioral benchmarks alone can overstate genuine understanding.

**Ziemińska 2026 (7.0, Strong Candidate, PDF not obtained — DSH paywalled, no OA copy found).** Metadata independently verified via the DOI resolver and the publisher page (title, author, journal, volume/issue, abstract all confirmed). Uses Gemini-2.5-Pro for rhetorical analysis of the Book of Revelation, validated against expert analysis (Pearson's r = 0.82), but reports "considerable variability, absence of reproducibility." This is the single closest found precedent to the thesis's actual research question (LLM applied to rhetorical analysis of a classical/canonical text, with an explicit validation-against-experts design) — its inaccessibility is a genuine gap in what could be reviewed, not a relevance gap.

**Attia et al. 2026 / Kinayat (8.5, Essential).** The single closest new find to this thesis's exact combination of "LLM + figurative/rhetorical language + Arabic." Evaluates 22 open- and closed-source LLMs on culturally grounded figurative Arabic (Egyptian idioms, multidialectal proverbs) across understanding, pragmatic-use, and connotation tasks, with human-agreement figures reported (models reach at most 85.58% agreement with human annotators even on idioms with 100% inter-annotator agreement). Not about classical poetry or named rhetorical elements specifically, but it is a rigorous, recent (EACL 2026), multi-model benchmark of LLMs on Arabic figurative language with quantified human-agreement metrics — directly strengthens the "LLM behavior on Arabic figurative/cultural language is measurably worse than on English, even for strong models" framing, and is useful as a second non-Arabic-poetry data point (after Al Kaabi et al. 2024) that LLMs underperform on Arabic figurative language specifically.

**Kühn, Mitrović & Granitzer 2024 (7.5, Strong).** A survey (preprint, submitted to ACM Computing Surveys) explicitly about rhetorical figures *beyond* metaphor/irony/sarcasm — the dominant figures in NLP literature — covering under-studied figures like antithesis, periphrasis-adjacent figures, and zeugma, and diagnosing why computational work avoids them (dataset scarcity, rule-based reliance). Useful directly for framing why periphrasis/كناية and antithesis/طباق are exactly the kind of "lesser-known," semantically demanding rhetorical figures the computational-rhetoric literature already recognizes as hard and under-served.

**Kühn & Mitrović 2024 (7.0, Strong).** A companion short paper (FigLang 2024, co-located with NAACL) distilling ten recurring challenges in computational detection of non-metaphor/irony rhetorical figures from a review of 40+ papers (2006–2024). Useful as a ready-made checklist against which to compare this thesis's own difficulty findings (e.g., periphrasis/كناية as a hard case) — several of its ten challenges (annotation subjectivity, context-dependence, scarce datasets) directly echo issues this thesis's own error analysis will likely surface.

**Qarah 2024 / AraPoemBERT (6.5, Strong).** Not about rhetorical elements or LLMs directly, but the most relevant "Arabic poetry NLP" infrastructure paper found: a BERT model pretrained exclusively on 2.09M verses of Arabic poetry, benchmarked on meter, rhyme, gender, and sentiment classification (not rhetorical-figure identification). Useful as evidence that large-scale annotated resources exist for Arabic poetry's formal features (meter, rhyme) but — consistent with the novelty-gap finding — not for its rhetorical/semantic features, reinforcing the claim that rhetorical-element identification remains a resource gap even as other Arabic-poetry NLP tasks mature.

**Fontana 2025 (6.5, Strong Candidate, theory reference — PDF not obtained, institutional repository blocks automated download with HTTP 403).** An English-language scholarly study of al-Sakkākī's *Miftāḥ al-ʿUlūm*, the classical codification of the tripartite balagha system (maʿānī/bayān/badīʿ) that underlies the rhetorical-element taxonomy this thesis evaluates LLMs against. Metadata (title, author, journal, volume, pages) independently verified via the author's institutional page (unibo.it) and corroborated by multiple independent search results; the PDF itself could not be downloaded (403 on the institutional repository URL), so it is listed as a verified citation without an obtained copy, same pattern as Ziemińska 2026.

**Harb 2020 (6.0, Strong Candidate, theory reference — book, no OA copy).** *Arabic Poetics: Aesthetic Experience in Classical Arabic Literature* (Cambridge UP), verified via the publisher's DOI landing page. A modern English-language scholarly study centered on al-Jurjānī's aesthetic theory (the "wonder" paradigm) underlying *Asrār al-Balāgha* — a reliable secondary source for citing al-Jurjānī's theoretical framework in English without relying on the unverifiable medieval primary text directly. No OA/PDF copy exists (Cambridge UP monograph); included as a verified metadata-only citation.

**Hayes & Krippendorff 2007 (6.0, Foundational reference).** Verified via the University of Pennsylvania Annenberg School repository (open PDF) and DOI resolution. The standard citable methods paper for Krippendorff's alpha as an inter-rater reliability statistic — usable in place of, or alongside, Krippendorff's own 1980/2018 book (which has no DOI/stable OA copy) wherever the thesis needs a single fetchable source for the alpha statistic's definition and justification.

**Cohen 1960 (6.0, Foundational reference).** *A Coefficient of Agreement for Nominal Scales*, Educational and Psychological Measurement 20(1):37–46. DOI 10.1177/001316446002000104 verified (resolves to the SAGE Journals page for this exact article, matching the citation found independently via multiple searches). The standard original source for Cohen's kappa, needed alongside Krippendorff's alpha if the thesis reports both statistics or explains why it chose one over the other.

**Lakoff & Johnson 1980 (6.0, Foundational reference).** *Metaphors We Live By* (University of Chicago Press), confirmed via the publisher's own book page. The foundational text for Conceptual Metaphor Theory — needed as the standard theoretical anchor if the thesis's literature review or discussion invokes CMT when discussing استعارة (metaphor) or مجاز more broadly, as several of the already-indexed LLM-metaphor papers (Hicke & Kristensen-McLachlan 2024 in particular) do.
