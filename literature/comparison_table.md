# Comparison table — DHQ Essential/Strong papers

| **Abd Alhadi, Hussein & Kuflik 2023** (DHQ 17.2) | Identify which verses/words realize each of 20 rhetorical-element groups (balagha) in classical Arabic poetry | Classical Arabic, pre-Islamic–Mamluk; study set 75 poems / 35 poets (887 vs 752 verses reported inconsistently), 77%/23% train/test split | 20 element groups from Cachia 1998 inventory; metaphor and periphrasis (kinaya) explicitly excluded as semantics-dependent; single annotator ("an Arabic literature researcher"); IAA not reported | Rule-based: string/position matching, AlKhalil2 morphological analysis, a self-built sentiment lexicon (8,765+/9,813- words) and antonym lexicon (453 pairs); no ML/embeddings/LLMs | Per-element precision/recall/F1 + TP/FP/FN (Tables 2–3); overall P 0.918 / R 0.902 / F 0.906 (abstract misreports F as 0.902, which is actually R); no baseline; Table 3 has internal inconsistencies (TP/FP/FN columns look swapped for positive antithesis and feints) | Context-free, surface-marked elements near-perfect; hard cases: positive antithesis (lexicon too small), feints (word polarity context-dependent), paronomasia, verbal congruence, implicit similes (excluded by design), antithesis broken by intervening words | Only 75/26,925 poems; some undiacritised; single annotator, no IAA; small test set (17 poems) inflates some element scores to 1.0; no baseline/CI; metaphor & periphrasis — the elements needing semantics — never evaluated |
| **Gutman 2024** (DHQ 18.3) | Classify words within manually-extracted figurative instances into 103 bottom-up topics, then build a co-occurrence network for literary interpretation of *Timon of Athens* | Early modern English; training corpus = 4–5 tragedies c.1605–1610 (1,388 figurative instances); case study = *Timon of Athens* (546 instances) | 103 topics built bottom-up, not fixed in advance; multi-label (up to 5 topics/instance); single annotator (the author); IAA "to be implemented in a future study" (not yet done) | DistilBERT-based neural classifier for topic assignment (66/33 train/test split); Gephi network analysis (centrality measures, ForceAtlas2, cluster detection) for interpretation | Classifier accuracy 72.94%, F-score 71.12 on held-out split; test-set size, per-topic scores, number of runs not reported; no baseline; detection of figurative instances itself not evaluated (done manually) | Figurative language harder than literal for topic modeling (no dominant topic per text); one sentence can contain multiple distinct metaphors, breaking sentence-level units; subjectivity of topic inventory and assignment; boundary ambiguity (which words count as part of the instance) | Single annotator for extraction and labeling, no IAA, no gold standard; 103 topics over 1,388 instances is a sparse label space — likely poor performance on rare topics, not shown; whether Timon's network used predicted or manual labels is unclear in the text; paper explicitly proposes LLMs (Llama/Gemini/ChatGPT) as future work, not tested |
| **Münz-Manor & Marienberg-Milikowsky 2023** (DHQ 17.3) | Manual + CATMA-assisted annotation of figurative devices in early Hebrew liturgical poetry (piyyut), then visualize distributions (ViS-À-ViS) to re-examine prior qualitative claims | Hebrew (late antiquity); 211 poems, 47,556 words from the Cairo Genizah; comparison corpora: Psalms, Pinchas the Priest poetry | Flat tagset: metaphor, metonymy, synecdoche, simile, applied by highlighter in the original dissertation and ported unchanged into CATMA; single original annotator; IAA not reported; authors argue against collapsing disagreement into one gold standard, favoring multi-annotator visualization | No automatic detection or ML/LLM — purely manual/CATMA annotation plus visualization tooling (Vue.js/Flask web app) | No metrics/baselines/accuracy — interpretive validation only (counts and percentages per genre, cross-checked against prior qualitative claims) | What counts as figurative is a hermeneutic, annotator- and time-dependent judgment call; original flat tagset limited analysis; quantity-vs-impression mismatches found (e.g., Hosha'not genre: 25% figurative density but perceived as low) | No automatic/LLM component at all, so no detection benchmark; single annotator, no IAA; comparison corpora (Psalms, Pinchas) given without sizes/annotation detail; final CATMA tagset not listed in the article |
| **Bender, Becker, Kiemes & Müller 2023** (DHQ 17.3) | (1) Sentence-level binary detection of "discourse referencing" in parliamentary speech; (2) classify the actor mention into 3 classes (explicit/metonymic/none) | German; German Bundestag plenary minutes 1949–2021 (~810k texts); experimental sample = random 6,000 sentences from one session week | Categories developed deductively (theory) then inductively (pilot data); two independent teams double-annotated all 6,000 sentences, disagreements resolved by a third annotator; Cohen's kappa (0–100 scale): Task 1 avg 65.02 (group range 57.44–72.87); Task 2a 84.35; phrase-extraction kappa not reported | Multinomial Naive Bayes baseline (bag-of-words) vs fine-tuned DistilBERT; three input variants (sentence/phrase/sentence+phrase) and three label settings for Task 2 | Majority baselines given (64.48% Task 1, 59.69% Task 2); micro F1: Task 1 — NB 80.30, DistilBERT 93.16; Task 2 Setting A — NB 78.81–81.45, DistilBERT 92.41–96.90 (up to 98.47 with classes merged); test-set sizes and number of runs/seeds not reported; NB and DistilBERT evaluated on different split proportions (not a clean comparison) | Implicit discourse referencing needs contextual/domain knowledge; lowest IAA was for the "simplest" binary task (sentence-level judgments still need full context); rare class (metonymy, 3.05% of data) hardest for models; error types: actor/metonymy confusion, false-positive actor predictions from named entities/pronouns | Pilot corpus, one phenomenon, one week of one parliament — generalization untested; very high F1 with unreported test-set size and a tiny metonymy class (59 gold instances) — fragile; phrase-input condition leaks gold-label location to the model (not realistic end-to-end detection); authors read high F1 as category-robustness evidence despite a low kappa — tension only partly discussed |
| **Zirker & Göggelmann 2023** (DHQ 17.3) | Annotate the ambiguous modality (epistemic/deontic/bouletic) and voice (narrator/character) of the modal verb "must" in *Emma*; "maximal annotation" — tag all plausible readings rather than disambiguate | English; Jane Austen's *Emma* (1816); 571 occurrences of "must" total, 323 occurrences in the annotated chapters | Tags: figure, role, modality (multi-label allowed); 19 students in 2 tool groups (CATMA, CorefAnnotator); Fleiss kappa on 22 instances / 3 groups: figure 0.67 (good), role 0.5 (moderate), modality 0.16 (poor); no gold standard — authors say one "would have to be developed" | Purely manual student annotation in a teaching context; no ML or LLM component; descriptive analysis only (counts of technical flaws, comparison tables, kappa) | No accuracy/precision/recall against a gold standard (none exists); only inter-annotator agreement reported, on a very small sample (22 instances) | Modality is the hardest dimension to agree on; figure/role comparatively easier; students tended to over-disambiguate rather than hold multiple readings; missed instances in direct speech; authors describe an "ambiguity of ambiguity" | Authors call the student annotations "not really of any use" for quantitative analysis; no gold standard; CATMA groups' data unusable (misapplied guidelines); tool and group confounded; kappa computed on only 22 instances; agreement conflates phenomenon difficulty with annotator training/novice status |

## Notes on gaps across the table
- No paper here evaluates an LLM on rhetorical/figurative-element identification. Two (Gutman, Bender) use a fine-tuned small transformer (DistilBERT); one (Abd Alhadi) is rule-based; two (Münz-Manor, Zirker) are purely manual.
- Inter-annotator agreement is reported in only two of five papers (Bender; Zirker), and in both cases the *coarsest* or *simplest* dimension got the *lowest* agreement — a pattern worth citing directly when motivating why "challenging" rhetorical elements need empirical, not assumed, difficulty measures.
- No paper reports span-level precision/recall with both strict and token-overlap criteria the way this project's `span_scoring.py` does; Abd Alhadi is closest (per-element P/R/F1 with raw TP/FP/FN) but at verse level, not strict span level.
