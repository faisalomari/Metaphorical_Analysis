# Limitations and Future Work

## 1. Ground truth from a single annotator

Every result in this thesis is evaluated against one expert's manual rhetorical analysis (Prof. Ali Hussein); no second annotator has independently labeled any poem, and no inter-annotator agreement figure exists anywhere in this project. This is the most consequential limitation of the present work, for two related reasons. First, the four elements studied here are not always cleanly separable even among expert readers of classical Arabic rhetoric — the boundary between trope and kenning, in particular, turns on a judgment about whether a literal reading remains plausible, which is precisely the kind of judgment on which trained annotators can reasonably disagree. Second, and more directly, every precision and recall figure reported in this thesis is implicitly a comparison against one person's judgment rather than against a consensus or an agreed-upon gold standard; a model's false positive against this ground truth is, in some unknown fraction of cases, better described as a defensible reading the annotator did not happen to mark, rather than a genuine model error. Without a second annotator and a measured agreement rate, this thesis cannot distinguish between these two possibilities, and every reported precision figure should be read with this qualification in mind, most acutely for the elements — kenning and antonymy — where the reported precision and recall are already the weakest.

Requesting a second annotator, and on which poems, is a question raised with the supervisor and not yet resolved as of this writing; see the open items recorded in the project's internal state document.

## 2. Corpus scope: five poems, one poetic tradition

All results in this thesis are drawn from five poems, three held out as a frozen test set and two used for development, all belonging to the classical Arabic qasida tradition and, within that, skewing toward the pre-Islamic and early Islamic period (Imru' al-Qais, Jarir, Abu Tammam, Al-Nabigha Al-Dhubyani). This is a narrow slice of the roughly fourteen centuries of classical and post-classical Arabic poetry in which these four rhetorical elements occur, and nothing in this thesis licenses a claim that the findings — the relative difficulty ordering of the four elements, the specific trope subtypes a refined prompt does or does not help, kenning's resistance to every prompting intervention tried — generalize beyond this tradition and period. Andalusian, Abbasid-era ornate, or Mamluk-era poetry, to name three traditions with markedly different rhetorical conventions and lexical registers, may present a different difficulty profile entirely.

The corpus is also small in absolute terms: 43 antonymy instances and 52 simile instances on the entire held-out test set, and as few as one antonymy instance on one of the two development poems. Several findings in this thesis — most explicitly the antonymy over-claiming pattern and the trope subtype breakdown — only became visible at the larger, three-poem test-set scale, after having been invisible or misleading on the two-poem development set; there is no principled reason to assume five poems is a large enough sample that a sixth poem could not reveal a comparable reversal.

## 3. Span-matching ambiguity

The scoring harness matches a model's predicted span against a ground-truth span using either a strict literal-substring criterion or a token-level overlap criterion (methodology chapter, §6.2), and the two criteria are reported side by side specifically because they disagree meaningfully, most visibly for antonymy, where Arabic morphological variation causes the strict criterion to undercount matches the token criterion recovers. Neither criterion is a fully adequate model of what should count as "the model found the right instance." The strict criterion is blind to legitimate morphological variation; the token-overlap criterion, being threshold-based on word-level overlap, can both under-credit a short but correct span (a single-word match against a longer multi-word ground-truth span may fall below the 0.5 overlap threshold) and over-credit a span that shares words with the ground truth for an unrelated reason. Neither criterion attempts to adjudicate whether a partially-overlapping span identifies the same *rhetorical function* the annotator intended, only whether the surface text overlaps by some measure; a full evaluation methodology for this task would likely need a semantically-informed matching criterion, or a second round of human adjudication on borderline matches, neither of which was in scope for this pilot cycle.

## 4. Element definitions and scope of the taxonomy

The four elements studied here, and their definitions, are drawn from one scholarly framing of classical Arabic rhetoric (a 53-category taxonomy collapsed to these four for this thesis's scope). Classical Arabic rhetoric (بلاغة) is not a single unified system with universally agreed category boundaries across scholarly traditions; a different rhetorical framework might define trope, kenning, or antonymy's boundaries somewhat differently, or might further subdivide categories this thesis treats as unified (the trope subtype analysis in the results chapter shows that even within one taxonomy, subtypes as different as verbal metaphor and single-word lexicalized metonymy behave very differently under the same prompt, which suggests the four top-level categories may themselves be coarser than is ideal for this kind of evaluation). Findings here are scoped to this specific taxonomy's definitions and should not be read as claims about "trope" or "kenning" as categories independent of the scholarly framework that defines them.

## 5. Prompt-only scope

Every model evaluated in this thesis was used zero-shot, through prompting alone; no fine-tuning, few-shot in-context example provision beyond the worked examples already present in the element definitions, or parameter-efficient adaptation of any kind was attempted. This is a deliberate scope decision for a pilot cycle intended to establish a baseline and a scoring methodology, but it means the ceiling this thesis reports for each element — most importantly kenning's — is a ceiling on what zero-shot prompting of general-purpose models can achieve, not a ceiling on what these models, or smaller specialized models, could achieve if fine-tuned on the available ground truth. Given that kenning's failure mode looks, on the evidence gathered here (§6 and §7 of the results chapter), more like a genuine gap in the model's ability to make the relevant literal-plausibility judgment than like a prompt-engineering-fixable friction, fine-tuning is a natural next step this pilot cycle did not attempt.

## 6. Two specific unsolved cases

Two findings in the results chapter identify particular sub-problems this thesis did not close, and both would benefit from evidence beyond what a prompting-only pilot can provide.

**مجاز مرسل** (metonymy realized as a single, conventionalized lexical item) is the one trope subtype the refined prompt does not improve at all — 85% missed under both the baseline and refined prompt, unchanged, while every other subtype with meaningful support (n≥15) improved by 17-21 percentage points. Because this subtype is, definitionally, a closed and largely enumerable set of conventionalized expressions rather than a compositional pattern a model can learn to recognize from a handful of worked examples, it is plausible that no amount of rule-based prompt refinement addressing general trope-vs-kenning boundaries would help, and that what is needed instead is direct lexical coverage — supplying the model with a list of known مجاز مرسل expressions as prompt context, rather than relying on it to infer the category from definitions and examples.

**Kenning's difficulty is broad and resistant to every intervention attempted.** It is the hardest element under both the baseline and refined prompt for every model on the frozen test set; per-element decomposition helped one of three models and made the other two measurably worse (§6 of the results chapter); per-verse calling produced the single largest positive effect observed in this pilot cycle for two of three models, but the effect's magnitude did not replicate reliably across poems, its absolute F1 even at its best (0.338) remains far below simile's typical ceiling, and it costs four to eight times more per unit of accuracy than the adopted method (§7, §9 of the results chapter). No configuration tried in this pilot cycle brings kenning to a level of accuracy comparable to trope or simile, and the evidence gathered here is consistent with this being a genuine capability gap rather than an artifact of any specific prompt structure tried so far.

## 7. Future work

**Fine-tuning.** As noted in §5, this pilot cycle is zero-shot throughout. A fine-tuned model — plausibly even a smaller, open-weight model — trained on the ground truth already collected across all five poems has not been attempted and is a natural next step, particularly for kenning, where the evidence gathered here points toward a capability gap rather than a prompting friction that further prompt engineering could be expected to close.

**Lexicon-augmented prompting for مجاز مرسل.** Given that this subtype is a closed, enumerable lexical class rather than a compositional pattern (§6 above), supplying the model with an explicit lexicon or list of conventionalized مجاز مرسل expressions as additional prompt context — rather than the general element definition and a handful of worked examples currently provided — is untested and specifically targeted at the one trope subtype the refined prompt leaves entirely unimproved.

**A fresh held-out poem for per-verse kenning validation.** The cross-poem replication check reported in the results chapter (§7) compared exactly two poems and found the per-verse kenning effect's direction held for two of three models while its magnitude varied by an order of magnitude between the two poems. Two poems is barely enough to establish that an effect can vary at all; a third poem would materially improve confidence in whether either poem's magnitude — or some value between them — should be treated as the more representative estimate, and is a comparatively low-cost way to firm up a finding that is otherwise the single most encouraging result this pilot cycle produced for kenning specifically.

**A properly scored test of the explanation-augmented prompt.** The illustrative explanation-augmented run described in the results chapter (§8) was a single run per model, on one poem, and evaluated only for whether decisions flipped relative to the standard prompt — never for whether the flipped decisions were themselves correct. A follow-up run under the full 2-run-majority protocol, scored against ground truth, would answer the practically more important question this illustrative sample could only gesture at: whether requiring a model to justify its positive calls is a net-positive or a net-negative prompting strategy for precision on this task, rather than simply an observation that it changes the model's behavior.
