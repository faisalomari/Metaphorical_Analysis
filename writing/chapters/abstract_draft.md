# Abstract (draft)

Classical Arabic rhetoric identifies figurative and structural devices — trope (مجاز), simile (تشبيه), kenning (كناية), and antonymy (طباق) — whose automatic detection has received little systematic evaluation. This thesis evaluates three general-purpose LLMs (Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5.4-mini, Gemini 3.5 Flash), zero-shot, on span-level identification of all four elements against one expert annotator's analysis of five classical poems, three held out as a frozen test set. A baseline prompt is compared against a refined prompt whose three added rules were derived from development-set error analysis, under a protocol requiring two-run majority agreement and both strict and token-level span matching.

The refined prompt substantially improves trope detection for all three models — GPT-5.4-mini's F1 nearly quadruples — concentrated in verbal and rational trope subtypes rather than the subtype a smaller development-set analysis had predicted. Simile is already well handled by all three models. Antonymy recovers better under token-overlap than strict matching, and all three models are found to systematically over-claim it, the reverse of trope and kenning's under-detection. Kenning remains resistant across every intervention attempted, including per-element and per-verse prompt restructurings; a cross-poem replication of the most promising restructuring shows its direction holding for two of three models while its magnitude varies by an order of magnitude between poems — generalizing, at the poem level rather than the run level, the same instability the two-run protocol exists to catch, and which this thesis argues is a caution for single-poem LLM evaluation methodology more broadly. All quantitative conclusions rest on the pooled, three-poem held-out set, not on any single poem's numbers.
