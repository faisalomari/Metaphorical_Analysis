# Data and Methods

## The corpus and its ground truth

The corpus consists of five classical Arabic poems: two by Imru' al-Qais (the Muʿallaqa, referred to throughout as Imru1, and a second, shorter poem, Imru2), and one each by Jarir, Abu Tammam, and Al-Nabigha Al-Dhubyani.

| Poem | Poet | Verses |
|---|---|---|
| Imru1 | Imru' al-Qais (Muʿallaqa) | 81 |
| Jarir | Jarir | 40 |
| Abu Tammam | Abu Tammam | 30 |
| Imru2 | Imru' al-Qais (poem 8) | 23 |
| Nabigha | Al-Nabigha Al-Dhubyani | 50 |

*Source: `experiments/pilot_3models/config.py`, `POEMS` registry.*

Every annotation in this study is manual rhetorical analysis by a single domain expert, Prof. Ali Hussein (Department of Arabic Language and Literature), against a fifty-three-category classical rhetoric taxonomy, mapped down to the four top-level elements evaluated here by substring matching on the taxonomy's primary and secondary labels (مجاز or استعارة mapping to trope; direct matches for تشبيه, كناية, and طباق). Exact-duplicate (span, element) annotation rows are deduplicated before any count is taken. **No second annotator has independently labeled any poem, and no inter-annotator agreement figure currently exists for this corpus.** A blind re-annotation protocol has been designed and partially built — an eighty-verse, poem-proportional stratified random sample, scored three ways (Cohen's κ and Krippendorff's α on verse-level presence/absence per element, plus strict and token-overlap span-agreement where both rounds agree an element is present) — but Prof. Hussein's re-annotation pass is not yet complete, so every κ/α/span-agreement figure below is `[TBD]`, not an estimate. *Source: `writing/paper_plan/iaa/agreement_protocol.md`, `methods_paragraph_draft.md`.* A separate, disagreement-enriched thirteen-verse set has also been assembled for a qualitative adjudication session with Prof. Hussein — model-alternate-reading cases, elements every model missed, model-flagged-but-unannotated cases, and cross-model disagreements — but it was deliberately selected for disagreement, not drawn at random, so it is reported only qualitatively, never as an agreement statistic.

Ground-truth support is uneven across both poems and elements, most severely for antithesis (as few as one instance on one development poem) and least severely for trope and periphrasis (the two most frequent elements corpus-wide, present in roughly half or more of all 224 verses — see the agreement-protocol's prevalence table: trope 62.5%, periphrasis 54.9%, simile 27.2%, antithesis 19.6% of verses, corpus-wide).

## The elements and subtypes

Four elements are evaluated at the top level, each defined by a short Arabic/English explanation plus four to five worked examples drawn verbatim from the project's canonical element-definition files (`data/elements/*.txt`, unchanged since the earliest phase of this project and reused unedited in every prompt variant). This paper uses the corrected English terminology (antithesis for طباق, periphrasis for كناية) adopted after a 2026-07-23 supervisor correction; the original English names used throughout the earliest experimental phase ("antonymy," "kenning") are a lexical-relation mislabeling and an unrelated Old Norse/Old English poetic device respectively, and are retained only when citing that phase's own frozen artifacts under their original names.

**Trope (مجاز)** — "a word used in a non-literal sense based on an associative relationship, accompanied by a contextual clue that prevents the literal meaning." Example: وَمَا ذَرَفَتْ عَيْنَاكِ إلَّا لتَضْرِبي / بِسَهْمَيْكِ في أعشَارِ قَلْبٍ مُقَتَّلِ. Trope is an umbrella covering at least three subtypes not distinguished at the top level in the primary evaluation: استعارة (metaphor, substitution by resemblance), تمثيل (analogy/extended metaphor, a whole-clause figurative comparison not reducible to one substituted word), and مجاز مرسل (metonymy/synecdoche, substitution by a non-resemblance relation — cause-for-effect, part-for-whole). Canonical مجاز مرسل examples used in the Phase 2 Config C prompt: «السماء تُمطر» ("the sky is raining," where the cloud, not the sky itself, is the actual cause) and «أراني أعصر خمراً» ("I see myself pressing wine," Qur'an 12:36, where what is literally pressed is grapes).

**Simile (تشبيه)** — "an explicit comparison between two things that share a similar quality, using a comparative marker such as 'like' or 'as'." Example: إذا ما الثُّرَيَّا في السَّمَاءِ تَعَرَّضَتْ / تَعَرَّضَ أثْنَاءِ الوِشَاحِ المُفَصَّلِ.

**Periphrasis (كناية)** — "conveys an indirect meaning by using words whose literal sense is not the intended purpose, while still allowing the literal meaning to remain possible." Example: وبَيْضَةِ خِدْرٍ لا يُرَامُ خَبَاؤُهَا / تَمَتَّعْتُ منْ لَهْوٍ بها غَيْرَ مُعْجَلِ.

**Antithesis (طباق)** — "the use of two words with opposite meanings within the same context to emphasize contrast and enhance the overall message." Example: تُضِيءُ الظَّلامَ بالعِشَاءِ كَأنَّهَا / مَنَارَة ُ مُمْسَى رَاهِبٍ مُتَبَتِّلِ.

*Source: `data/elements/{Trope,Simile,Kenning,Antonymy}.txt`, `GLOSSARY.md`.* A finer-grained, twenty-three-subtype taxonomy (built from Prof. Hussein's course materials and his 2025 *Journal of Arabic Literature* glossary) was evaluated separately in a supplementary recall-only screen (see Results); it is not part of the primary four-element evaluation.

A later analysis found that three of every element file's four or five worked examples are, in fact, literal verses from the three TEST poems this paper's primary evaluation scores — a held-out-set exposure present in every prompt variant used in this paper, discussed as a limitation below rather than corrected retroactively, since the frozen evaluation (below) cannot be re-run against TEST under a new prompt without violating this project's own held-out discipline. *Source: `results/phase2_chatlike/example_gap_analysis.md`.*

## Models

Three general-purpose, closed-weight LLMs were evaluated in the primary zero-shot evaluation: Claude Sonnet 4.6 (Anthropic, `claude-sonnet-4-6`), GPT-5.4-mini (OpenAI, `gpt-5.4-mini`), and Gemini 3.5 Flash (Google, `gemini-3.5-flash`), all called at temperature 0 with an instruction-only JSON output format (no provider-native structured-output enforcement, for cross-provider comparability). A fourth model, Fable 5 (`claude-fable-5`), was added only in the supplementary disentanglement experiment described below. A separate, single-model follow-up (Gemini 3.5 Flash, later partially replicated with GPT-5.4-mini) was used for the twenty-five-subtype recall screen. *Source: `experiments/pilot_3models/config.py`; `writing/chapters/methodology.md` §5.*

## Prompting design: what is zero-shot and what is not

**The primary evaluation (below, "frozen TEST results") is entirely zero-shot.** No model was shown any in-context demonstration beyond the four or five worked examples already embedded in the static element-definition files loaded into every system message (the same files used throughout, not inserted per prompt variant). Two prompts were compared: a **baseline** system message (element definitions plus a strict per-verse JSON output schema) and **v4_combined**, a refined prompt adding three rules derived from development-set error analysis — a trope-versus-periphrasis boundary rule (the literal reading is impossible/rejected for trope, remains a plausible alternative for periphrasis, explicitly including non-literal verb usage), a marker-independence rule (an explicit simile particle does not exempt the rest of a verse from being checked for the other three elements), and a multi-figure rule (a verse may contain more than one element, or more than one instance of the same element). Four individual variants and two ablation combinations were tried on the development set before `v4_combined` was adopted as the single prompt carried forward to the frozen test evaluation, for all three models.

**Phase 2's variants are not zero-shot in the same narrow sense, and this must be stated plainly.** Config C, a two-stage, per-verse pipeline (Stage 1: free-form Arabic reasoning on Fable 5, full poem context, one verse per call; Stage 2: extraction into the unmodified strict JSON schema on GPT-5.4-mini), removes the no-explanation constraint and grants the model reasoning space the primary evaluation's schema-bound calls do not — a genuine format change, not merely a different model. Config A isolates the model variable alone (Fable 5 substituted for Claude Sonnet 4.6, `v4_combined` unchanged). Config B isolates the terminology variable alone (Claude Sonnet 4.6, `v4_combined` with only the English element names corrected and the مجاز block annotated with its three subtypes and the two canonical مجاز مرسل examples above). All three configurations remain zero-shot in the sense that no worked demonstration beyond the static element-definition files was added; what changes across A/B/C is model, terminology, and output format, not the presence of in-context examples. A separate, designed-but-not-run few-shot proposal was abandoned once the TEST-leakage finding above showed its planned demonstration source was contaminated.

**The twenty-five-subtype recall screen (Phase 3) is one-shot, not zero-shot**, and a one-shot-versus-three-shot comparison was run as part of that same phase: each subtype's prompt contains exactly one worked example (one textbook example per subtype, drawn from Prof. Hussein's own course materials and glossary), and a separate three-shot variant (the same textbook example plus two additional real-corpus examples, held out of testing) was compared against it for the thirteen subtypes with enough volume to spare two examples. *Source: `results/phase3_subelements/MEETING_SUMMARY.md` §4, §8b.*

## Span-level scoring

Every evaluation in this paper scores at the **span level**, never at the coarser verse-level exists/does-not-exist judgment used in the project's earliest harness. The earliest, verse-level scoring method was found to produce a measurable artifact on multi-instance verses (a verse with two ground-truth instances scored identically to one with a single instance), and re-scoring the same single-poem pilot data with a corrected, span-aware harness left trope's F1 unchanged but *lowered* periphrasis's apparent score once multi-instance verses were counted correctly — confirming the artifact was real and element-specific. *Source: `writing/chapters/methodology.md` §6.1.*

Two independent span-matching criteria are reported side by side for every result in this paper: a **strict** criterion (literal substring overlap between the model's predicted span and a ground-truth span) and a **token-overlap** criterion (word-level overlap above a 0.5 threshold, with a partial correction for attached و/ف clitics). The two criteria diverge meaningfully for antithesis specifically, where Arabic morphological variation (e.g. النُّوّامِ against the ground truth's نَوَّمُوا — different inflected forms of one root) causes the strict criterion to undercount matches the token criterion recovers.

**Every configuration in this paper is run at least twice, and the two-run majority decision is the primary reported metric; single-run numbers are never the basis for a comparative claim.** This discipline was adopted after an early single-run comparison showed one model's periphrasis F1 apparently increasing eightfold under a candidate prompt rule; a second run brought the honest, majority-vote figure down to roughly a quarter of the apparent gain. Raw true-positive/false-positive/false-negative counts are reported alongside every precision/recall/F1 figure throughout this paper, specifically so a result built on a small denominator remains visibly distinguishable from one built on a larger, more stable count.

## The DEV/TEST discipline

Before any prompt design began, the five poems were partitioned into a development set (DEV: Imru2, Nabigha) and a held-out test set (TEST: Imru1, Jarir, Abu Tammam). All prompt design and iterative refinement for the primary evaluation was conducted exclusively against DEV. TEST was evaluated exactly once, in a single frozen run comparing the baseline prompt against `v4_combined`; after that run, TEST was not re-run, re-tuned, or re-scored under any new prompt, including two supplementary DEV-only follow-up experiments (per-element decomposition and per-verse granularity restructuring) conducted after the TEST run was frozen, both evaluated on DEV poems only for exactly this reason. All of Phase 2's disentanglement configurations (A/B/C) and the entire Phase 3 subtype screen are likewise DEV-only or, for Phase 3, pooled across DEV and TEST poems without touching the frozen TEST evaluation's own numbers. *Source: `writing/chapters/methodology.md` §3; `PROJECT_STATE.md` §2.*

## Difficulty analysis

Three complementary analyses characterize *where* difficulty concentrates, beyond the headline per-element precision/recall/F1: a trope subtype breakdown (pooled across the three TEST poems, by ground-truth secondary label), a cross-model, span-level re-score of the twenty-five-subtype recall screen for two models, and a qualitative, interpretive account (the Discussion section's marker-explicitness hypothesis) of what predicts whether a model's success on a given element or subtype is high or low — namely, whether some feature of the line (an explicit particle, a lexical opposition, or a semantic anomaly) forecloses the literal reading for the model, rather than how conceptually sophisticated the figure is held to be in the classical taxonomy.
