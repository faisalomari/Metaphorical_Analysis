## 2. Top issues (ranked)

### Issue 1 — Internal contradiction: did antithesis "resist" the rule-based system, or did it "handle it well"?
**Location:** final paragraph of the "LLM benchmarks… beyond digital humanities" section: *"...together they explain why antithesis and periphrasis resisted Abd Alhadi, Hussein and Kuflik's (2023) rule-based system..."*
**Problem:** This directly contradicts RQ2 later in the same document: *"...the more surface-marked elements, such as antithesis, their rule-based system already handles well."* Per `2023_AbdAlhadi_RhetoricalElementsArabicPoetry.md`, antithesis was **not** excluded — only metaphor and periphrasis were explicitly bracketed out as "too semantic" (p.11 ¶59). Antithesis was attempted by the rules, with per-type scores of F1 0.777 (negative antithesis) and F1 0.500 (positive antithesis, flagged in the notes as a genuine hard case: "lexicon too small"). "Resisted" overstates this into full exclusion, putting antithesis in the same bucket as metaphor/periphrasis, which the paper itself (correctly) does not do in RQ2.
**Verdict: Overstated / self-contradictory.**
**Suggested replacement:** *"Neither paper covers periphrasis specifically, and neither touches Arabic; together they explain the structural reasons why periphrasis and metaphor — rather than antithesis, which Abd Alhadi, Hussein and Kuflik's (2023) rule-based system already handles reasonably well — were the elements that paper's own authors judged out of reach for rule-based matching."*

### Issue 2 — "Antithesis, a close typological relative of periphrasis" is unsupported
**Location:** same paragraph: *"...named rhetorical figures beyond metaphor, irony, and sarcasm (among them antithesis, a close typological relative of periphrasis)..."*
**Problem:** No note or index file anywhere calls antithesis and periphrasis typological relatives, and nothing in `2024_Kuhn_LesserKnownRhetoricalFiguresSurvey.md` supports it — that note explicitly warns: *"periphrasis (كناية) does not appear anywhere in this paper's figure list... Do not overstate this paper as directly validating periphrasis-specific difficulty."* Structurally, antithesis (opposition between parallel terms) and periphrasis (saying something indirectly while the literal reading stays possible, per `PROJECT_STATE.md` line 350) are different rhetorical mechanisms with no stated typological kinship in any source consulted.
**Verdict: Unsupported — appears invented.**
**Suggested replacement:** *"...named rhetorical figures beyond metaphor, irony, and sarcasm (among them antithesis, a figure this paper's own corpus also evaluates)..."* — drop the "close typological relative" claim entirely, or replace with a citation if one can be found.

### Issue 3 — Qarah (2024)/AraPoemBERT: "era" classification is not what the only verified source says
**Location:** opening section: *"...reports strong results on meter, rhyme, era, and gender classification..."*
**Problem:** The only verification trail for this paper is `RELATED_WORK_INDEX.md`'s one-line description (no deep note exists): *"benchmarked on meter, rhyme, gender, and sentiment classification"* — sentiment, not era. There is no deep-read note to adjudicate which is correct, and the PDF could not be rendered in this session (pdftoppm missing). This is either a transcription slip (era for sentiment) or a correction the draft writer made from an unrecorded check of the actual PDF — either way, it is currently unverifiable from this project's own evidence trail and the two records disagree.
**Verdict: Unsupported pending a direct PDF check.**
**Suggested replacement (pending verification):** *"...reports strong results on meter, rhyme, gender, and sentiment classification..."* (to match the only currently-verified source), **or** open `literature/related_work/non_dhq/2024_Qarah_AraPoemBERT.pdf` directly and confirm which task list is accurate before finalizing either wording.

### Issue 4 — Harb (2020) and Fontana (2025): draft adds specificity beyond the verified one-liner
**Location:** opening section: *"...treats figuration as a cognitive and aesthetic achievement rather than ornamental excess, and supplies much of the conceptual vocabulary later writers use to argue that a figure succeeds or fails (Harb, 2020)"* and *"...organizes this inventory into the tripartite division of ma'ani, bayan, and badi' that still structures how the tradition's rhetorical devices are taught and catalogued (Fontana, 2025)."*
**Problem:** Neither PDF was obtained (Harb: no OA monograph copy; Fontana: institutional 403). The only verified content is `RELATED_WORK_INDEX.md`'s one-liners: Harb = "al-Jurjānī's aesthetic theory (the 'wonder' paradigm)"; Fontana = "the classical codification of the tripartite balagha system… that underlies the rhetorical-element taxonomy this thesis evaluates." The draft's specific claims — "cognitive and aesthetic achievement rather than ornamental excess," "supplies much of the conceptual vocabulary later writers use to argue that a figure succeeds or fails," "still structures how the tradition's rhetorical devices are taught and catalogued" — are plausible but go beyond what either index entry actually verifies, despite `02_related_work_notes.md` explicitly claiming the draft "do[es] not add detail beyond what the index states."
**Verdict: Overstated relative to the project's own verification standard** (not necessarily false — just not independently checked).
**Suggested replacement:** *"Al-Jurjani's theory of poetic 'wonder,' most fully developed in his Asrar al-Balagha, supplies a scholarly anchor for this tradition's aesthetic vocabulary (Harb, 2020). Al-Sakkaki's Miftah al-'Ulum then organizes this inventory into the tripartite division of ma'ani, bayan, and badi' (Fontana, 2025)."* — i.e., trim the elaborated claims down to what the index one-liners actually support, or obtain and read the sources before keeping the fuller wording.

### Issue 5 — Tarkka et al. (2026): "Finnish parliamentary speech" and "precision, recall, and F1" are not in the verified description
**Location:** "LLM-in-DH literature" section: *"...comparison of a BERT-based classifier against human questionnaire responses on Finnish parliamentary speech... pairs standard precision, recall, and F1 reporting with an explanation-analysis layer (SHAP)..."*
**Problem:** Tarkka et al. has no deep-read note (Strong Candidate, not one of the five Essential papers deep-read per `DHQ_LITERATURE_INDEX.md`'s own disclosure). The only verified description is: *"Compares a BERT classifier with human questionnaire responses on party affiliation, and uses SHAP to show which cues each relies on; the model picks up personal style and rhetoric that humans miss."* Neither "Finnish" nor "parliamentary speech" nor "precision, recall, and F1" (as opposed to just F1, which `dhq_format_guide.md`'s structural table does confirm: "5 tables (per-party F1, cue phrases)") appears in anything independently verified in this project.
**Verdict: Partially supported, partially unverified elaboration.** "Finnish" is a reasonable inference (author names/DHQ context) but not confirmed; "precision, recall, and F1" overstates what's confirmed (F1 only).
**Suggested replacement:** *"Tarkka et al.'s comparison of a BERT-based classifier against human questionnaire responses on party affiliation is the closest DHQ precedent in genre to this paper's own design: a rigorously reported, recently published model-versus-human study that pairs per-party F1 reporting with an explanation-analysis layer (SHAP) showing which textual cues the model actually relies on, and finding that the model tracks stylistic and rhetorical cues that human readers largely miss (Tarkka et al., 2026)."*

### Issue 6 — Periphrasis "looks easiest to define on paper": not supported by PROJECT_STATE.md
**Location:** "DH precedents" section: *"...a direct structural parallel to a finding already visible in this project's own pilot work on periphrasis, where the category that looks easiest to define on paper has proven to be among the hardest to apply consistently."*
**Problem:** `PROJECT_STATE.md` §6.1 documents periphrasis/Kenning difficulty as a **run-to-run noise/instability** problem (an "8x" apparent gain on one run that halved on a second run; Gemini's persistently unstable Kenning behavior), not as a case of a category that "looks easiest to define on paper." Nothing in `PROJECT_STATE.md` characterizes periphrasis as looking definitionally simple — if anything, periphrasis is already flagged project-wide (per `GLOSSARY.md`/CLAUDE.md) as the semantically hardest element, the one Abd Alhadi et al. excluded as too semantic for rules in the first place. The "looks easiest on paper" framing appears to conflate this project's periphrasis-measurement-noise finding with Bender et al.'s unrelated "simplest category, lowest agreement" finding, which is about a *different* phenomenon (discourse-referencing in German) and a *different* kind of "simple" (coarse binary label granularity, not semantic transparency).
**Verdict: Unsupported / likely a mischaracterization of this project's own results.**
**Suggested replacement:** *"This is a direct structural parallel to this project's own pilot work on periphrasis, where small-sample noise and run-to-run instability have already produced a misleadingly large apparent effect that halved on replication — the same lesson Bender et al.'s coarsest-category/lowest-agreement finding points to: that difficulty must be measured, not read off a category's surface description."*
