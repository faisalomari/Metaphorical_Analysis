# Proposal Reuse Map

**Purpose**: map `writing/proposal/` (the approved thesis research proposal) against what the thesis has actually become, to determine what can be reused verbatim, what needs updating, and what is now outdated, ahead of drafting the DHQ paper. Nothing here edits the proposal itself (frozen, per instruction) or any file under `writing/chapters/` or `writing/proposal/`.

**Headline finding, before anything else**: the proposal and the thesis as actually executed are about **two different research projects**. The proposal (all versions, v0 through v6) is titled "Discovering Hidden Tashbeeh in Arabic Poetry" / "Combining Classical Rhetorical Theory with State-of-the-Art AI Techniques for Discovering Hidden Tashbīh in Arabic Poetry as a Case Study," and its entire method is: rule-based detection of explicit simile (تشبيه) → binary-labeled dataset → fine-tuning AraBERT to classify poems as containing/not-containing tashbīh (96% reported accuracy on that classification task) → a planned next phase of manually transforming explicit similes into implicit ones and fine-tuning a second classifier to detect the implicit forms. The thesis as actually built (`PROJECT_STATE.md`, Phases 1–3) studies a different, four-element inventory (trope/مجاز, simile/تشبيه, periphrasis/كناية, antithesis/طباق — simile only one of the four, and not the one the pilot work later focused hardest on), via a completely different method (zero-shot/few-shot prompting of general-purpose LLMs — Claude, GPT, Gemini, Fable — evaluated with span-level precision/recall/F1 against expert ground truth), with no AraBERT fine-tuning anywhere in the executed work. The new paper's title ("Exploring the Potential of Large Language Models to Identify Challenging Rhetorical Elements") reflects the executed project, not the proposal's title. This is the single fact that governs every judgment below: the proposal's *motivation and domain framing* (classical Arabic rhetoric, tashbīh/balāgha, why this matters, the Haifa REI lineage) remains usable, but its *method, dataset-construction plan, and reported results* (AraBERT, 96% accuracy, the 29,265-poem transformation plan) do not transfer and must not appear in the new paper as if they were the method used.

## (b) Per-section reuse assessment

**Introduction.** *Reusable as-is*: none of the prose — the entire section is built around explicit-vs-implicit tashbīh as the single object of study, with the worked example "كأنّ سيوفهم نجومٌ في الليلة الظلماء" vs. "سيوفهم نجوم في الليل" as its hook. *Must be updated*: the paper's actual novelty claim (four-element inventory including periphrasis/كناية and trope/مجاز, the elements Abd Alhadi et al. 2023 explicitly excluded from rule-based detection — not simile, which that system already handles) must replace the tashbīh-only framing. *Outdated*: the AraBERT fine-tuning pipeline description (rule-based labeling → binary classifier → "96% accuracy"), the 29,265-poem manual-transformation plan, and every RQ in the proposal's §3 (see (c)) are all specific to the abandoned method and do not belong in new writing, even as historical framing, since the paper's new title and focus no longer claims tashbīh — classical or implicit — as its subject at all. One piece of motivating language is still accurate and reusable: the general observation that Arabic poetry's rhetorical richness is "deeply intertwined with the culture, language, and identity of the Arab people" and that classical rhetoric (balāgha) treats these devices as foundational, structured stylistic systems — this is generic enough to still be true and is already better served by `02_related_work_v2.md`'s own opening paragraph (Harb 2020, Fontana 2025 citations), which should be preferred over reusing the proposal's own unsourced version of the same claim.

**Background and Related Work.** *Reusable as-is*: the proposal's own citation of the Haifa Arabic Rhetoric Identifier (REI) — "a rule-based system for detecting rhetorical devices in Arabic poetry... over 29,000 poems spanning 10 centuries... only a subset... manually annotated" — is the same REI/Abd Alhadi lineage the executed thesis's related-work section already develops in far more detail and with corrected framing (`02_related_work_v2.md` §"Prior automatic approaches," citing Abd Alhadi, Hussein and Kuflik 2023 directly, with the specific exclusion-of-metaphor/periphrasis claim the proposal's related work does not make — the proposal's own related-work paragraph on REI, written before that 2023 DHQ paper's exact findings were available to it in this form, is a strictly weaker version of material `02_related_work_v2.md` already supersedes). The proposal's description of classical tashbīh structure (mushabbah/mushabbah bihi/wajh al-shabah/particle) is accurate classical-rhetoric content but is about simile specifically, which is not where the new paper's own difficulty findings concentrate (periphrasis and the harder trope subtypes are) — reusable only as general domain color, not as load-bearing content. *Must be updated*: nothing from the "Advances in NLP for Arabic" or "Detailed Overview of Arabic NLP Tools" subsections is needed in the new paper, whose method does not use AlKhalil/MADAMIRA/BAMA/SAMA/Farasa/AMIRA at all (the executed thesis's LLM pipeline has no morphological-analysis preprocessing step). *Outdated*: the Belinkov & Glass (2019), Ibrahim & Elghazaly (2013)/RST, and Khan & Rahman (2025) paragraphs are generic Arabic-NLP/rhetorical-structure color not cited anywhere in the executed project's own, far more targeted related-work draft (`02_related_work_v2.md`), which already independently covers Belinkov & Glass-adjacent territory with more current, directly relevant sources (Fuoli et al. 2025, Kühn et al. 2024, Attia et al. 2026). Recommendation: do not reuse the proposal's Background section's prose; `02_related_work_v2.md` is already a complete, better-sourced replacement.

**Research Goals and Questions.** *Reusable*: none of RQ1/RQ1.1–1.4 — all are tashbīh-specific ("What are the linguistic and semantic characteristics that distinguish implicit tashbīh from other poetic statements such as metaphors?", "How effective is a transformation-based method in creating... implicit tashbīh for training data augmentation?"). *Outdated in full*: see (c) for the direct RQ-by-RQ comparison against `gap_statement.md`'s actual RQ1–RQ3.

**Tools and Methods / Initial Experiments and Results.** *Reusable*: none of the method description (rule-based Java→Python conversion, AraBERT fine-tuning, 5%/95% train/test split, the specific tool list) or the reported results (95.71% accuracy / 95.46% precision / 95.99% recall on binary tashbīh classification) — this is a different system solving a different, narrower problem (tashbīh-presence classification, not span-level multi-element identification) with a different method (supervised fine-tuning, not zero-shot prompting) on a different evaluation design (no DEV/TEST split as the executed project defines it, no span-level scoring, no multi-model comparison). None of these numbers should appear in the new paper as if they were baseline results for the executed work; if the AraBERT pilot is mentioned at all, it should be flagged explicitly as an earlier, abandoned pilot direction, not as part of this paper's own experimental record.

**Planned Research and Future Work / Contribution.** *Reusable*: none of the specific plans (manual/semi-automatic simile-marker removal to synthesize implicit tashbīh, balanced three-way explicit/implicit/non-tashbīh dataset). *Outdated*: all of it, for the same reason as above — this was the plan for a project that did not end up being executed as proposed.

## Final note on §(b)'s overall reuse verdict

Across all four proposal sections, the only content that transfers into the new paper with minimal change is: (i) the general motivating observation that classical Arabic rhetoric is a rich, understudied computational target (better served by `02_related_work_v2.md`'s already-written, better-sourced version of this claim than by reusing the proposal's own prose); (ii) the Haifa REI/Abd Alhadi lineage as the project's direct predecessor (same conclusion); and (iii) one reference (Hussein 2015) worth adding to the Works Cited that was not previously tracked.
